# ScrollArea

A vertical scrolling area with a thin scrollbar drawn over its right edge, taking no width from the content: the page (`WorkspaceShell`), `TaskList`, `BoardColumn`, `VirtualCardGrid`, the sidebar, a sheet's body and the editor's suggestion menus (`SlashMenu`, `MentionMenu`) (README "Scrollbars"). The native scrollbar is hidden.

- **Scrollbar.** An 8px strip on the right edge holding a 4px `border-strong` thumb with round ends, 2px in from the edge and 4px from each end (at least 16px tall). It isn't drawn when nothing overflows, and it measures again whenever the content changes size, not only the area: a page swapping a tall child for a short one (Home for a list page, a project's Overview for its Tasks tab) leaves no stale scrollbar.
- **Showing.** It fades in (`duration-normal`) while the area scrolls or the pointer is over it, and fades out 0.8s after scrolling stops or once the pointer leaves.
- **Dragging.** The scrollbar is a control: drag the thumb to scroll, or click the track above or below it to jump there. The wheel, trackpad and keyboard scroll the area as usual.
- **Keyboard.** While it overflows, the area takes keyboard focus (the arrow keys then scroll it), shown as a 1px `ring` outline inside its edge. A click on its content focuses it too, and then draws no ring, not even once a key is pressed (the browser would show one then, so every shortcut after a click would frame the area).
- **One direction.** Vertical by default: content wider than the area is cut off rather than scrolled sideways. `orientation="horizontal"` turns it round for a row wider than the area (the `Board`'s columns): it scrolls sideways under the same scrollbar along its bottom edge (8px tall, the thumb 2px up from the edge and 4px from each end), and content taller than the area is cut off.
- **Use.** Give the area a height, or let a flex column size it (`ScrollArea` is a flex column whose viewport takes the spare height). The children go in the area's content, one element inside the viewport: put the content's own layout (padding, flex, gaps) on `contentClassName`. The content is at least as tall as the area, so a flex column's `flex: 1` child fills it, and as tall as its children beyond that; scrolled down it keeps to the area's width, and scrolled sideways it is the area's height and as wide as its children. A row pinned with `position: sticky` stays pinned inside the viewport.
- **Filling it.** A child that fills the area and scrolls itself (a list under a toolbar) takes `flex: 1 1 0; min-height: 0`, not a basis of `auto`: the content grows with what is in it, so a child sized by its own content would grow to its whole list and scroll the area instead.
- **A widget that scrolls itself.** A virtualized list scrolls in the viewport: pass `viewportRef` (the virtualizer's scroll element) and `viewportProps` for what the scrolling element carries as the widget (`role="grid"`, `aria-*`, `tabIndex`, focus handlers, a `data-slot`). `viewportProps.tabIndex` replaces the viewport's own (focusable while it overflows): `TaskList` keeps itself one tab stop.
- **`data-overflow-y-start`:** the root carries it while the content is scrolled from the top, so a pinned header can cast `shadow-pinned` (the sidebar's does).

**Base UI:** `ScrollArea.Root`, `ScrollArea.Viewport`, `ScrollArea.Content` (which measures the overflow again as it resizes), `ScrollArea.Scrollbar` (vertical, or horizontal) and `ScrollArea.Thumb`. Styles key off `data-scrolling`, `data-hovering` and `data-orientation` on the scrollbar and `data-overflow-y-start` on the root.
