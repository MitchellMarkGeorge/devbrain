# TaskRow

One task in a strokeless list: no line beneath, a `row-height` (48px) row with 8px corners that takes the `row-selected` tint on hover and when selected.

- **Leading control.** The status icon is a button: click it for the `StatusMenu`, then `1`, `2` or `3`. A synced task's status is its provider's (`isSynced`: its link is `synced`, not `detached`), so its icon is the same 24px slot with no button, no hover tint and no menu, and `S` opens nothing on it; a click there opens the task. The selection checkbox sits in a strip to its left and shows on hover, on keyboard focus, and on every row while any row is selected.
- **No keys of its own.** `S`, `X` and ↵ on a focused row are the app keymap's list commands, run through `TaskList` (`menuOpen`, `onSelect`, `onOpen`), so another keymap can rebind them.
- **Title first.** The name in 14px `foreground-strong`, then pills:
  - a synced task's `SourcePill`: the provider's logo and the key (`external.key`: "[Linear] ENG-124"), so where the task comes from and its key read as one unit;
  - on a subtask, its parent (`parentTask` as an `EntityPill` of type `parent`: corner arrow, status icon, parent title), left off on a row drawn under its parent in a tree (`tree.depth` > 0), where the guide points at the parent, and with `showParent={false}` (a task's own Subtasks section: the parent is the task it is on);
  - on a parent task, the subtask count in a pill (`subtaskProgress`: `ProgressRing` and "3/4"). Given `subtasksShown` and `onSubtasksShownChange` (the tasks pages' list, for a parent with subtasks drawn under it), the pill is the button that shows and hides them: a 12px chevron after the count, down while they are shown and right while they are hidden, with `aria-expanded`. It opens no hover card, since the subtasks the card would list are the rows under it, a click away; a click toggles rather than opening the task, and → / ← on the row do the same. Elsewhere (the board, Home, a project's overview) it stays a plain pill.
- **When the row runs out of room,** the pills on each side stack like a deck (the last in full, the ones behind showing a 26px edge with their icon) and spread out on hover or focus, drawn over the end of the title on the row's own ground (never past the start of the title) so nothing in the row moves; the row keeps its height. Stacking and spreading animate in `duration-normal`: as the row narrows or widens, or a stack is pointed at, the pills behind grow or shrink between full width and their edge and their labels fade; a spread stack's ground fades in first (`duration-fast`). A row draws its first level still. The title shortens only after that. The row measures itself and stacks as far as it needs to (`stack="auto"`, the default); `stack="right"` stacks the pills after the title, and `stack="both"` also stacks the key with the subtask or parent pill; a side with one pill never stacks. In Figma this is TaskRow's `pills` variant (`spread`, `stacked right`, `stacked both`).
- **Right side.** `EntityPill` chips (the task's one `link`, an event or a note, left off with `showLink={false}`, then the project with its folder icon in the project's colour, so the project is the pill a stack shows in full; `showProject={false}` leaves it off, for a list of one project's tasks; the task sheet's narrow Subtasks section passes both `false`, as a subtask's project and link are mostly its parent's), `PriorityIcon`, and the due date from `useFormat().formatListDate`, always the date and never relative ("Jul 1"; the month and year when it is not this year's: "Dec 2025"), 60px right-aligned (the widest, "May 2027", fits) with tabular numbers so the dates line up down the list, as Linear's do. Whether it is today or overdue is said by its colour, not its words: the colour comes from the status and the date (`dueTone`): `date-overdue` for an open task due before today, `date-today` for one due today, else `subtle-foreground`; a closed task's date is never coloured.
- **No due date.** An undated task's 60px date slot is blank at rest, so a list of undated tasks stays quiet; the sort puts them last by due date. With `onDueDateChange`, the slot shows a `SetDueDateButton` (a quiet 24px calendar-plus and "Due", named "Set due date", opening the `DatePicker`) while the row is pointed at or focused, and keeps it while the picker is open. The button is drawn over the slot's end rather than in it, so an undated row's priority and pills stay in line with a dated row's. A closed or synced task never shows it: there is nothing to date, or the provider owns the date. The sheet, the task page and the create dialog show an unset due date as a "Due date" pill instead, as every unset property is.
- **Anatomy.** A grid of named areas: `check`, `status`, `title`, `side` (`compact` leaves out `check`). The 24px status slot starts 36px from the row's edge; the 16px checkbox sits before it, 8px from the edge and 12px from the status slot, in an 18px slot it keeps while hidden, so showing it moves nothing. 10px between the status, the title and the right side; 8px between the right side's parts.
- **States.** Rest; hover (`row-selected`, the same tint as selected); selected (`row-selected`, title in `row-selected-foreground`; neighbouring selected rows join into one block); keyboard focus (the hover tint with a 1px `ring` outline around the whole row, no halo, as Figma draws it); completed (title in `muted-foreground`, not struck through; the priority still shows); overdue, worked out from the status, the due date and `now` (`dueTone`): the date in `date-overdue`, the status icon unchanged. A subtask under its parent is drawn **in a tree** (below).
- **In a tree** (`tree`, which `TaskList`'s `nested` gives each row; the tasks pages' list and grouped views): a subtask drawn under its parent is indented 30px per level (`tree.depth`), so its status icon's glyph starts exactly where its parent's title does, and guide lines in `border-strong` join it to its parent. From the centre of the parent's status icon a 1px line runs down and turns into each subtask's status icon, 4px short of it: rounded into the last one (└), a straight tick off the line into the others (├). A stem runs down from a parent's own status icon while its subtasks are drawn under it (`tree.open`), and a straight line at each level above carries an ancestor's line past a deeper subtask (`tree.continues`). The lines meet across rows and never overlap, since the translucent colour would draw darker where they did. The checkbox stays in the strip, in line with every other row's. A subtask whose parent is not above it is at depth 0 and keeps its parent pill.
- **`compact`** (Home's tasks, as Figma's Home draws them): 44px, no selection strip and no checkbox, the status icon 12px in and 16px at the right. Home has no selection, so it takes no `onSelect`.
- `task`: core's `TaskListItem` as it is, typed `TaskRowData` (`Pick<TaskListItem, 'id' | 'title' | 'status' | 'priority' | 'dueDate' | 'subtaskProgress' | 'project' | 'link' | 'parentTask' | 'external'>`); the row derives everything it draws from it. `selected` is the row's own prop, not the task's. `status` controls the status (otherwise the row keeps `task.status` and its own picks); `onStatusChange` gets a `TaskStatus`. `stack`: `'auto'` (the default: the row measures itself), or `'none' | 'right' | 'both'` to pin a level. `tree`: `{ depth, continues, open }` (`TaskRowTree`), worked out from rows in tree order by `treeOf`. `subtasksShown` / `onSubtasksShownChange(shown)`: the subtask count as a show/hide button. Use it inside `TaskList`, which handles selection and focus.

**Hover cards.** Each pill is a `PillHoverCard`, except the subtask count when it is the show/hide button: pointing at it for about 400ms (or focusing it) opens its `EntityHoverCard` below it, from the `PillHoverCardsProvider` around the page (the key shows the task's state at its provider and when it synced, the parent its priority, due date and subtasks, the subtask count its subtasks with their status, the link and the project their own cards). It opens on a spread stack's pills too, and the stack stays spread while the card is open (the pointer may go on to it), folding once it closes.

**Clicks that don't open the task.** Its own controls (the status button, the checkbox, its pills, links) and anything it opened, such as the status menu, a date picker or its context menu: those are portalled elsewhere in the page, but React still bubbles their clicks up to the row, which ignores a click whose target is outside it.
