# TaskRow

One task in a strokeless list: no line beneath, a `row-height` (48px) row with 8px corners that takes the `row-selected` tint on hover and when selected.

- **Leading control.** The status icon is a button: click it for the `StatusMenu`, then `1`, `2` or `3`. A synced task's status is its provider's (`isSynced`: its link is `synced`, not `detached`), so its icon is the same 24px slot with no button, no hover tint and no menu, and `S` opens nothing on it; a click there opens the task. The selection checkbox sits in a strip to its left and shows on hover, on keyboard focus, and on every row while any row is selected.
- **No keys of its own.** `S`, `X` and ↵ on a focused row are the app keymap's list commands, run through `TaskList` (`menuOpen`, `onSelect`, `onOpen`), so another keymap can rebind them.
- **Title first.** The name in 14px `foreground-strong`, then pills:
  - a synced task's `SourcePill`: the provider's logo and the key (`external.key`: "[Linear] ENG-124"), so where the task comes from and its key read as one unit;
  - on a subtask, its parent (`parentTask` as an `EntityPill` of type `parent`: corner arrow, status icon, parent title), left off on a row drawn under its parent in a tree (`tree.depth` > 0), where the guide points at the parent, and with `showParent={false}` (a task's own Subtasks section: the parent is the task it is on);
  - on a parent task, the subtask count in a pill (`subtaskProgress`: `ProgressRing` and "3/4"). Given `subtasksShown` and `onSubtasksShownChange` (the tasks pages' list, for a parent with subtasks drawn under it), the pill is the button that shows and hides them: a 12px chevron after the count, down while they are shown and right while they are hidden, with `aria-expanded`. It opens no hover card, since the subtasks the card would list are the rows under it, a click away; a click toggles rather than opening the task, and → / ← on the row do the same. Elsewhere (the board, Home, a project's overview) it stays a plain pill.
- **When the row runs out of room,** the pills give way first and the title last, and the title is never hidden.
  1. The pills on each side of the title stack: each slides under the one after it (opaque, on the row's own ground), down to its icon, so the last pill of a group is on top and whole.
  2. The last pill of each group shortens its label, down to its icon; a key or a subtask count stays whole.
  3. The title shortens, down to 48px and no further.

  The priority and the date never give way. Pointing at a group of pills (or focusing one of them, or one's hover card being open) spreads that group back out, as a group, and the title gives way to it instead, down to its 48px; pointing at the rest of the row only tints it. If even that is too little room the group spreads as far as there is room. A spread group covers all of where it was stacked, gaps included, so the pointer that spread it stays on it. This is the row's flex layout alone: the title, each pill's slot and the side's parts are items of one flex line, with the pills set to shrink long before the title, so nothing is measured and nothing is animated. In Figma, TaskRow's `pills` variant (`spread`, `stacked right`, `stacked both`) draws an earlier stack.

- **Right side.** `EntityPill` chips (the task's one `link`, an event or a note, left off with `showLink={false}`, then the project with its folder icon in the project's colour, `showProject={false}` leaves it off, for a list of one project's tasks; the task sheet's narrow Subtasks section passes both `false`, as a subtask's project and link are mostly its parent's), `PriorityIcon`, and the due date from `useFormat().formatListDate`, always the date and never relative ("Jul 1"; the month and year when it is not this year's: "Dec 2025"), 60px right-aligned (the widest, "May 2027", fits) with tabular numbers so the dates line up down the list, as Linear's do. Whether it is today or overdue is said by its colour, not its words: the colour comes from the status and the date (`dueTone`): `date-overdue` for an open task due before today, `date-today` for one due today, else `subtle-foreground`; a closed task's date is never coloured.
- **No due date.** An undated task's 60px date slot reads "No date" (`NO_DATE_LABEL`) in the dates' `subtle-foreground`, so an empty slot is never mistaken for a missing value; the sort puts them last by due date. With `onDueDateChange`, "No date" gives way to a `SetDueDateButton` (a quiet 24px calendar-plus and "Due", named "Set due date", opening the `DatePicker`) while the row is pointed at or focused, and keeps it while the picker is open. The button is drawn over the slot's end rather than in it, so an undated row's priority and pills stay in line with a dated row's. A closed or synced task never shows it, and keeps "No date": there is nothing to date, or the provider owns the date. The sheet, the task page and the create dialog show an unset due date as a "Due date" pill instead, as every unset property is.
- **Anatomy.** One flex line: the checkbox, the status, the title and its pills, then the side (its pills, the priority and the date) pushed to the right; each pill sits in a slot, which is what narrows when the pills stack (`compact` leaves out the checkbox). The 24px status slot starts 32px from the row's edge; the 16px checkbox sits before it, 8px from the edge, in a slot it keeps while hidden, so showing it moves nothing. 8px (`space-2`) between the checkbox, the status, the title and its pills, the right side, and the right side's parts.
- **States.** Rest; hover (`row-selected`, the same tint as selected); selected (`row-selected`, title in `row-selected-foreground`; neighbouring selected rows join into one block); keyboard focus (the hover tint with a 1px `ring` outline around the whole row, no halo, as Figma draws it); completed (title in `muted-foreground`, not struck through; the priority still shows); overdue, worked out from the status, the due date and `now` (`dueTone`): the date in `date-overdue`, the status icon unchanged. A subtask under its parent is drawn **in a tree** (below).
- **In a tree** (`tree`, which `TaskList`'s `nested` gives each row; the tasks pages' list and grouped views): a subtask drawn under its parent is indented 28px per level (`tree.depth`), so its status icon's glyph starts exactly where its parent's title does, and guide lines in `border-strong` join it to its parent. From the centre of the parent's status icon a 1px line runs down and turns into each subtask's status icon, 4px short of it: rounded into the last one (└), a straight tick off the line into the others (├). A stem runs down from a parent's own status icon while its subtasks are drawn under it (`tree.open`), and a straight line at each level above carries an ancestor's line past a deeper subtask. What a row draws at each level is its prefix, `tree.cells`, as the `tree` command prints one: `tee` (├), `elbow` (└), `line` (│) or `none`. The lines meet across rows and never overlap, since the translucent colour would draw darker where they did. The checkbox stays in the strip, in line with every other row's. A subtask whose parent is not above it is at depth 0 and keeps its parent pill.
- **`compact`** (Home's tasks, as Figma's Home draws them): 44px, no selection strip and no checkbox, the status icon 12px in and 16px at the right. Home has no selection, so it takes no `onSelect`.
- `task`: core's `TaskListItem` as it is, typed `TaskRowData` (`Pick<TaskListItem, 'id' | 'title' | 'status' | 'priority' | 'dueDate' | 'subtaskProgress' | 'project' | 'link' | 'parentTask' | 'external'>`); the row derives everything it draws from it. `selected` is the row's own prop, not the task's. `status` controls the status (otherwise the row keeps `task.status` and its own picks); `onStatusChange` gets a `TaskStatus`. `tree`: `{ depth, cells, open }` (`TaskRowTree`), worked out by `treeOf` from the rows' `depth`, in tree order (core's `tasks.tree`). `subtasksShown` / `onSubtasksShownChange(shown)`: the subtask count as a show/hide button. Use it inside `TaskList`, which handles selection and focus.

**Hover cards.** Each pill is a `PillHoverCard`, except the subtask count when it is the show/hide button: pointing at it for about 400ms (or focusing it) opens its `EntityHoverCard` below it, from the `PillHoverCardsProvider` around the page (the key shows the task's state at its provider and when it synced, the parent its priority, due date and subtasks, the subtask count its subtasks with their status, the link and the project their own cards). It opens on a stacked pill too, and its group stays spread while the card is open.

**Clicks that don't open the task.** Its own controls (the status button, the checkbox, its pills, links) and anything it opened, such as the status menu, a date picker or its context menu: those are portalled elsewhere in the page, but React still bubbles their clicks up to the row, which ignores a click whose target is outside it.
