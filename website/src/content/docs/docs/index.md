DevBrain is a local-first, personal second brain for developers: a desktop app that gives you a
clear picture of what you're working on and when. Your **tasks**, **projects**, **notes** and
**calendar** live in one window, linked to each other. Your **Linear** issues and **Google
Calendar** events appear beside them.

## Why DevBrain

A developer's work is spread across an issue tracker, a calendar, a notes app and their head. The
question DevBrain is built around is simple:

> If you woke up tomorrow with no memory of your work, could you ramp back up?

With DevBrain you can. It keeps your work in one place, connected, so you can tell what you're
doing today, what's next, and what you worked on last week:

- **Four things, connected.** Everything is a [task](/docs/tasks/), a [note](/docs/notes/), a
  [project](/docs/projects/) or an [event](/docs/calendar/), and each knows the others it belongs
  with: the meeting a task came out of, the spec it implements, the project it moves forward.
  [How your work connects](/docs/connections/) explains the links.
- **Technical and professional.** The bug you're fixing and the notes from your onboarding, a code
  snippet and your 1:1 notes: it all goes in the same place.
- **Easy to put things in, easier to find, easy to take out.** Capture a task in one line from
  [Home](/docs/home/), find anything with [search](/docs/search/), and take everything out as CSV,
  JSON or Markdown with [export](/docs/import-export/).
- **Yours alone.** DevBrain is for your own work, not a team's. It runs on your computer, works
  offline, and keeps your data in files you own.
- **Out of your way.** It's quiet and quick, with a [keyboard shortcut](/docs/keyboard-shortcuts/)
  for most things. What it puts in front of you, on Home and in the calendar's Up next, comes from
  your dates and the connections you make.

It draws on the ideas of Getting Things Done and the second-brain method, without asking you to
learn either.

## How it works

- **Everything is on your computer.** A [workspace](/docs/workspaces/) is a folder on your disk. It
  holds a database for your tasks, projects and events, and a Markdown file for each note.
- **No account, no server.** There is no DevBrain account or cloud, and no telemetry. DevBrain only
  goes online to sync the [integrations](/docs/integrations/) you connect, and it talks to Linear and
  Google directly.
- **Sync brings things in; it doesn't send them back.** Linear issues and Google events are mirrored
  into DevBrain and stay read-only there. You change them in Linear or Google Calendar.
- **Your notes are plain Markdown.** You can read them in any editor, and copy, import or export
  them as `.md` files.

[Data and privacy](/docs/data-and-privacy/) explains this in more detail.

## Start here

1. [Install DevBrain](/docs/install/) on macOS, Windows or Linux.
2. [Create your first workspace](/docs/getting-started/) and take the tour of the window.
3. [Connect Linear or Google Calendar](/docs/integrations/), or
   [import the tasks and notes](/docs/import-export/) you already have.

## What's in a workspace

| Area                        | What it holds                                                                |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Home](/docs/home/)         | Today at a glance, and a box to capture a task, note or event in one line    |
| [Tasks](/docs/tasks/)       | Lists, groups and boards, with statuses, priorities, dates and subtasks      |
| [Projects](/docs/projects/) | Tasks and notes grouped toward a goal, with dates and progress               |
| [Notes](/docs/notes/)       | Rich text that reads and writes Markdown, saved as files                     |
| [Calendar](/docs/calendar/) | Your events by month, week or day, including the Google calendars you choose |
| [Search](/docs/search/)     | <kbd>⌘K</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl K</kbd> on Windows and Linux) searches all of it    |
