---
title: How your work connects
description: 'How DevBrain connects your work: projects hold tasks and notes, a task links to one note or event, meeting and task notes, subtasks and mentions.'
---

DevBrain has four kinds of things: **tasks**, **notes**, **projects** and **events**. On their own,
each is like what you'd find in any app. What makes DevBrain a second brain is how they link: open
any one, and the others it belongs with are a click away.

The links are deliberately few, so there's always one clear place for something.

## Projects hold tasks and notes

A **project** is a goal you're working toward. A task can be in one project, and so can a note. The
project's page brings them together, with its progress worked out from its tasks.

- Put a task in a project with its **Project** property, or type `#Project` when you
  [capture it](/docs/home/#quick-capture).
- Put a note in a project with **+ Add to project** under its title.

## A task links to one note or one event

A task can point to **one note** (the spec it implements, the decision it follows from) or **one
event** (the meeting it came out of). Choose **+ Add › Event or note** on the task.

From the other side, the note shows the tasks that point to it in its **Tasks** section, and the
event lists them as **Linked tasks**. So the follow-ups from a meeting, or the work a spec calls for,
are all in one place.

## Tasks and events have a note of their own

Besides those links, a task can have a **task note**, for the working notes that don't belong in
its description, and an event can have an **event note**, for the meeting's notes. Each is an
ordinary note, named after its task or event, and linked to it:

- **Add a task note** on a task's page.
- **Start Note** on an event, to write while the meeting happens. See
  [meeting notes](/docs/calendar/#meeting-notes).

A note is linked to at most one task or one event this way. **Link to** in a note's **…** menu
changes which.

## Subtasks follow their parent

A subtask is in its parent's project, and shares its parent's linked note or event. Move a task under
another with **Make subtask of…**, and it takes the parent's project and link in place of its own,
with its own subtasks.

## Mentions link anything in your writing

In any note or description, type <kbd>@</kbd> to link a task, a note or an event right in the text.
A mention is a link, not a relationship: it doesn't change which project or note anything belongs
to, so you can mention as freely as you like.

Each item keeps track of what mentions it: a task, note, project or event shows **Mentioned in**,
the notes and tasks that link to it, so you can go back from it to where it was discussed (see
[Mentioned in](/docs/notes/#mentioned-in)).

## Where the links show

- **On the item:** its links are pills under the title. Click one to open what it links to. The
  last pill says how many items mention it.
- **On Home:** what's due today and the meetings coming up, from your dates and links.
- **In search:** every task, note, project and event, in one search.
- **When you export:** tasks keep their project and subtasks, and notes their project. Links to
  events and notes aren't exported.
